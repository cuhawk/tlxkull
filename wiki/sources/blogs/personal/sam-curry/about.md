**Sam Curry** (born October 17, 1999) is an American [ethical hacker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethical_hacker "Ethical hacker"), [bug bounty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bug_bounty "Bug bounty") hunter, and cybersecurity entrepreneur.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Curry#cite_note-ganz-1)[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Curry#cite_note-2) He has uncovered high‑impact security flaws across a range of technologies and industries. Notably, he led a 2022 project that exposed remote‑control vulnerabilities affecting 20 car manufacturers,[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Curry#cite_note-lakshmanan-3) and in 2024 he and a colleague revealed a weakness that allowed bypassing of Transportation Security Administration (TSA) airport security screenings.[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Curry#cite_note-4)

## Early life and education

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Sam_Curry&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Early life and education")\]

Curry grew up in [Omaha, Nebraska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omaha,_Nebraska "Omaha, Nebraska") and began hacking at age 12, initially by modifying online video games.[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Curry#cite_note-5) He received his first bug‑bounty payout at 15 and by 18 had earned over US$500,000 in rewards.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Curry#cite_note-ganz-1)

## Career

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Sam_Curry&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Career")\]

### Palisade Security

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Sam_Curry&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Palisade Security")\]

In 2018 Curry founded the security consulting group Palisade Security, through which he reported serious vulnerabilities in companies including [Apple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apple_Inc. "Apple Inc."), [Starbucks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starbucks "Starbucks"), [Atlassian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlassian "Atlassian"), and [Tesla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tesla,_Inc. "Tesla, Inc.").[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Curry#cite_note-curry-6)[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Curry#cite_note-7) In September 2022, Google mistakenly wired Curry US$249,999.99, an error he publicized and later returned to the company.[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Curry#cite_note-8)

### Automotive research

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Sam_Curry&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Automotive research")\]

In December 2022, Curry led research that exploited telematics endpoints from SiriusXM to remotely unlock, start, and locate vehicles made by Porsche, Mercedes‑Benz, Ferrari, Toyota, and others.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Curry#cite_note-lakshmanan-3)

### Domain registry vulnerabilities

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Sam_Curry&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: Domain registry vulnerabilities")\]

In June 2023, Curry and collaborators demonstrated critical flaws in the infrastructure of multiple country-code top-level domains (ccTLDs), including [.ai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/.ai ".ai") and [.ly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/.ly ".ly").[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Curry#cite_note-9)

### Loyalty‑program vulnerabilities

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Sam_Curry&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: Loyalty‑program vulnerabilities")\]

In August 2023, Curry, Ian Carroll, and Shubham Shah revealed API flaws in the [Points.com](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Points.com&action=edit&redlink=1 "Points.com (page does not exist)") loyalty platform that could grant attackers virtually unlimited airline miles and administrator access to dozens of travel rewards programs.[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Curry#cite_note-newman-10)

### 2023 federal detainment

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Sam_Curry&action=edit&section=7 "Edit section: 2023 federal detainment")\]

Upon returning from Japan on 15 September 2023, Curry was detained by IRS-CI and DHS agents at Washington Dulles International Airport and served a grand-jury subpoena linked to a cryptocurrency phishing investigation. The subpoena was withdrawn days later.[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Curry#cite_note-11)

### Cable modem vulnerabilities

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Sam_Curry&action=edit&section=8 "Edit section: Cable modem vulnerabilities")\]

In 2024 Curry discovered an authorization bypass in Cox Communications' device management APIs that allowed attackers to remotely reconfigure or access millions of customer modems.[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Curry#cite_note-12)

### Airport security vulnerability

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Sam_Curry&action=edit&section=9 "Edit section: Airport security vulnerability")\]

In August 2024, Curry and Ian Carroll disclosed a flaw in the TSA's Known Crewmember (KCM) system that could allow unauthorized access through airport security checkpoints and even cockpit credentials.[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Curry#cite_note-13)

### Recruitment‑platform vulnerabilities

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Sam_Curry&action=edit&section=10 "Edit section: Recruitment‑platform vulnerabilities")\]

In July 2025 a _Wired_ investigation revealed that Curry and Ian Carroll had exposed vulnerabilities in McDonald's AI hiring platform, which allowed access to personal data from millions of job applicants.[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Curry#cite_note-14)

## Conference speaking

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Sam_Curry&action=edit&section=11 "Edit section: Conference speaking")\]

Curry has presented at [DEF CON](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DEF_CON "DEF CON"), [Black Hat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Hat_Briefings "Black Hat Briefings"), [Kernelcon](https://kernelcon.org/), and [NULLify](https://nullify.uno/) security meet‑ups.[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Curry#cite_note-15)[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Curry#cite_note-16) At DEF CON 32 in 2024, Curry gave a talk titled "Hacking Millions of Modems and Investigating Who Hacked My Modem".[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Curry#cite_note-17)
