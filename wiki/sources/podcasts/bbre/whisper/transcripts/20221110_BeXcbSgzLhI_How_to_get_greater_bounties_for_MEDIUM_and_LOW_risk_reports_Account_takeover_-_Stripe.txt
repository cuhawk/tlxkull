The first thing that I recommend you is to work with the program, not against the program.
I know that you report vulnerabilities to make bounty and so do I. This is my main goal.
But when writing the report, literally imagine the customer of the company you are reporting to,
how they can get harmed and write this in the report. Don't write the report about a hypothetical
user, but write about the very user of this company and what can happen with them.
With that said, you should be working with the team, but you should not expect the team to work with you.
You should expect that the triage will not make any work more than necessary to process your report.
Assume they won't fix your minimal errors in your proof of concept or in your explanation.
Sometimes, of course, they do, but often they don't have too much time to process your report,
so they will do it as quickly as possible. So don't assume they will like cooperate with you,
but assume that you have to do all the work yourself. And sometimes that will not be the case
and then you will be positively surprised. The second thing is to not treat the triageers or the team
as idiots and don't try to trick them into triaging your report with higher severity than it actually
deserves. Because if you set too high severity, they will just first treat you as someone that's not
so serious or not competent and they might downgrade the risk of your report even lower than it actually
should be. If your vulnerability has some limitations, let's say an unlikely user interactions where,
for example, the user has to click three times, acknowledge this in the report. Imagine what would
you say if you were the triager and you would try to downgrade your own report. Think about all the
arguments that you could use and try to address them while writing the report. Because if you don't
do this, what will happen is the triager will think about it anyway at some point. The triager will reply
to you asking for these things and then you have to address them anyway and you are in much better
position if you address them straight away. This gives the triager the information that you are aware
of those limitations but you reported the bug anyway and probably the triager won't get back to you with
your own arguments. Instead, the triager is more forced to ask the team if they want to triage the
vulnerability which has lower risk level because of these prerequisites. Of course, this won't magically
triage your bug just because of that. But also, if you don't put this in your report, neither they will
accept the report so you pretty much can't loosen this. Hoping that someone will not notice those
prerequisites, I think it's just naive. I'm definitely guilty of writing a lazy report when I know the bug
