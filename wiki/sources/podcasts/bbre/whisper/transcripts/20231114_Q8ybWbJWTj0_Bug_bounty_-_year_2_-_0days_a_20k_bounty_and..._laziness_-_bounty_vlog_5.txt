Bug bounty is easy. It's what we think when we first hear about it and we see thousands
of dollars being awarded for not so complex bugs. But is it really? I'm not a person that
started their cyber security career 20 years ago and I didn't hack since primary school.
I was a regular dude who studied computer science, started working as a pentester and
two years ago I quit to do bug bounty and create content. I'm sharing with you details of my
journey and in this video I will tell you about my second year of bug bounty with precise
number of reports and earnings. Enjoy!
One day I logged into my WordPress website and I saw an alert one. At first I thought
it's some kind of joke by a plugin developer but turned out that one of you tried an XSS
payload in the search field on my website and it didn't work for you but it did trigger the
XSS on the backend. It was a zero day in Monster Insights plugin used by over 3 million websites
which makes it one of the most popular WordPress plugins. A bug like this is great because not only
you can report it to the developers of the plugin but also to every single website that uses the
plugin and has a bug bounty program so we can get multiple bounties for one bug. And since this
plugin was so popular I knew it's worth thousands of dollars. I sort of wanted to find out who actually
found it and let you know that you just found the bug but it was just impossible to track who
initiated this search on my website. So I decided to do it myself and I collaborated with my friend
David who I knew does a lot of bug bounty automations which by the way are now available as a product called
VDoc. In total we found 19 websites with bug bounty programs using this plugin and we made... we made zero dollars from it.
The problem was that it was quite a complex blind XSS because it triggered in a very specific functionality
and also dependent on the analytics backend used. We just didn't get blind XSS pingbacks to XSS Hunter.
To be honest I'm still not exactly sure why but in hindsight I should have spent more time using this
plugin with different analytics backends to create a payload suitable for every single integration.
One way or another it was a huge disappointment because it in theory this bug affected 3 million
websites and I couldn't even make one dollar out of it. But it definitely made me hungry for more scalable
zero days like this. Later I was hacking on Discord, an open source forum and among other bugs I found an XSS
