So, following on from this, tools are really the next critical part of context engineering for an agentic system.

So, when it comes to APIs for AI tools, Model Context Protocol, or MCP, immediately comes to mind.
Hopefully, some of you have heard of it, but if you haven't, it's a widely adopted API in the industry, and it's growing a large number of useful capabilities, and it kind of makes it the obvious choice for exposing tools to agents at the moment.
It's often analogized as the USB-C for AI.
It's a universal connector that makes it easy to connect your different AI platforms to external tools and data.
In the protocol, the MCP server provides the context.
That could be access to your burp tools, such as Repeater or Intruder, or something like the files on your computer.
And then the MCP client on the other end then pulls that context in so the AI can actually use it.
So, MCP has some really desirable capabilities built in, such as tool discovery and interoperability.
So, this means that agents can query which tools are available and what they can do without requiring hard-coded integrations.
It also supports continuous streams of data.
So, for security testing, this could enable long-running tasks, such as crawling or brute-forcing, where partial results can be consumed immediately by the agent.
And finally, it has fine-grained controls.
MCP lets tool providers declare what tools and actions are available.
For offensive tooling like burp, this creates an extra layer of assurance and control when exposing potentially destructive operations to those AI agents.
