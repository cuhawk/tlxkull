## About

The **PortSwigger Top 10 Web Hacking Techniques** is an annual
community-curated ranking of the most novel, impactful, and broadly
useful offensive web-security research published the prior year. The
project was originally started by Jeremiah Grossman in 2006, ran through
2015, and was revived in 2017 by PortSwigger (notably James Kettle), who
has run it every year since. It is widely regarded as the definitive
record of the field's annual progress.

The process has three phases. First, anyone may **nominate** research
published in the target calendar year — blog posts, conference talks,
whitepapers, write-ups — through a public submissions form. Second, the
community **votes** on the nominations to produce a shortlist of roughly
15 entries. Third, a small **expert panel** (typically including James
Kettle plus several rotating senior researchers) re-ranks the shortlist
based on novelty, generality, and demonstrated real-world impact, and
publishes the final Top 10 with commentary. The canonical landing page
is <https://portswigger.net/research/top-10-web-hacking-techniques>.

## Notes

**Techniques that became staple bug classes.** The Top 10 is an
unusually reliable leading indicator of the next 12-24 months of
bounty pay-outs. Several entries crystallized whole new categories:

- *Practical Web Cache Poisoning* (Kettle, 2018 #9) and *Cache Poisoning at Scale* (Youstin, 2021 #6) — birthed the modern web cache poisoning / deception families that still pay out heavily, including 2024's ChatGPT ATO via wildcard cache deception.
- *Prototype Pollution Attacks in NodeJS Applications* (Arteau, 2018 #7) → *Exploiting Client-Side Prototype Pollution in the wild* (s1r1us, 2021 #4) — pollution went from theoretical to standard bug-bounty checklist item.
- *Practical Web Cache Poisoning* and *HTTP Desync Attacks* (Kettle, 2019 — won that year but appears via the smuggling chain) → *H2C Smuggling* (2020) → *HTTP/2: The Sequel is Always Worse* (2021) → *Browser-Powered Desync* (2022) → *Unveiling TE.0* (2024) → *Playing with HTTP/2 CONNECT* (2025). Request-smuggling research is the single longest-running thread in the list.
- *Dependency Confusion* (Birsan, 2021 #1) — became the supply-chain bug class.
- *Smashing the state machine* (Kettle, 2023 #10) — race-condition single-packet attack became standard kit overnight (Turbo Intruder).

**Recurring authors.** The list is dominated by a small group:

- **James Kettle** (PortSwigger) — appears in 2018, 2019 (winner), 2021, 2022 (twice), 2023. Web cache, HTTP smuggling, race conditions.
- **Orange Tsai** — 2018, 2019 (twice), 2020, 2021, 2024 (winner, twice). Parser-logic, RCE-via-SSRF, ProxyLogon, Confusion Attacks, WorstFit. Arguably the most prolific entrant overall.
- **Sam Curry** — 2019 (winner), 2020, 2022, 2023. Bug-bounty practitioner research; characteristically broad-surface.
- **Frans Rosén** — 2018, 2022 (winner). OAuth and "modern web" composition bugs.
- **Masato Kinugawa** — 2019, 2023. Browser-trust-boundary work (Google XSS, Teams RCE).
- **Paweł Hałdrzyński** — 2019, 2020. .NET XSS / WAF evasion.
- **Arseniy Sharoglazov** — 2018, 2020. XXE / Exchange surface.
- **Jake Miller** (Bishop Fox) — 2020 (winner), 2021. Smuggling + JSON interop.
- **Piotr Bazydło** — 2023, 2025. .NET deserialization → SOAPwn.

**Bigger structural trends.**
1. *Protocol-level bugs eat the list.* From 2020 onward, the winners and runners-up have been disproportionately HTTP/1↔HTTP/2↔HTTP/3 parser desync, header smuggling, cache-key confusion, and protocol multiplexing — the ratio of "logic bug on an endpoint" entries has dropped.
2. *OAuth / SSO remains the most reliable recurring web app surface.* At least one OAuth-flavoured entry has appeared in 2021, 2022 (winner), 2024 (twice), 2025-adjacent (redirect-loop SSRF).
3. *Browser-side / client-side leaks are growing.* XS-Search (2018 #1), Cross-Site Leaks (2019), XSS-Leak / ETag length leak (2025) — side-channel leak research is now a permanent fixture rather than a novelty.
4. *Bug-bounty practitioner research is rising relative to academic / conference research.* Earlier years over-indexed on conference talks; 2022-2024 saw more independent-blog entries (Detectify Labs, Synacktiv, Bishop Fox, Snyk, Bugcrowd, Watchtowr).
